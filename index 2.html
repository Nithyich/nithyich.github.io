04 — Approach

How I work

I use practical governance to shorten the distance between a problem appearing and the right person being able to make a well-informed decision.

01

Name the decisions before the meetings

Start with the decisions delivery must make and work back to ownership. A forum without a decision list becomes a status update; status updates do not unblock work.

02

Define what “ready” means in writing

Set entry and exit criteria before the deadline. Clear readiness standards protect quality when schedules become compressed.

03

Make scope and entitlement explicit

Reconcile what was contracted, what is being delivered, and what is being consumed early enough to turn surprises into routine decisions.

04

Size risk against exposure, not anxiety

Rank risk by plausible impact and likelihood. Every material item needs an owner, mitigation, and review date.

05

Report the same numbers to everyone

Maintain one source for status, risk, and dependencies so commercial, delivery, and risk teams can work from the same picture.

06

Escalate on a threshold, not a mood

Agree in advance what crosses the line—such as schedule slip, severity, or impact—so escalation is consistent and timely.

07

Leave it runnable by someone else

Document cadence, criteria, roles, and handoffs so the governance process survives the person who established it.
